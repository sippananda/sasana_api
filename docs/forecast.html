How the 5-day forecast is made

Section 6 of the haze page. For planning only.

Forecast for planning only. Closure decisions use the real DOE readings at 2:00 pm.

1In short

  • The forecast comes from CAMS, the European haze model (Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service, run by ECMWF).
  • CAMS forecasts fine dust (PM2.5) for 5 days, in squares of about 44 km.
  • We turn PM2.5 into the DOE API, for the 2:00 pm reading of our four stations.
  • CAMS reads too low in peat haze. So we correct it with our own station readings.
  • The direction of change (clearing or building) is more reliable than the exact number.

Malaysia time. ECMWF says each run is ready within about 10 hours. Our runs come later, so each one gets the newest CAMS run. The morning run includes today's 2:00 pm reading. The evening run starts from tomorrow.

3From CAMS to our forecast number

  1. 24-hour average. For each day, take the 24 hours of CAMS PM2.5 that end at 2:00 pm. The DOE API is also a 24-hour average, so one hour alone would not compare.
  2. Correction. CAMS reads too low in peat haze, so its value is corrected (section 4):
    • Same day (the morning run): start from the newest real hourly reading of each station. Add only the change that CAMS expects until 2:00 pm. The DOE API is a 24-hour average, so at 7:00 am about 17 of the 24 hours of the 2:00 pm value are already measured.
    • Later days: multiply by a mix of two factors: the newest real ratio of the station, and the haze factor. The newest ratio counts for about half on the next day, and less on each day after.
  3. DOE formula. Turn PM2.5 into API with the DOE table (the version of 14 October 2026 on the APIMS website).
  4. Average of the four stations. Batu Muda, Petaling Jaya and Shah Alam lie in one CAMS square. They still get slightly different numbers, because each starts from its own real reading.

4Correction with our own readings

  • For each past day, we compare the real 2:00 pm reading with what CAMS said for that day.
  • Correction factor = real PM2.5 ÷ CAMS PM2.5. Real PM2.5 comes from the DOE API, by the DOE table in reverse.
  • The forecast uses the middle value (median) of the newest 10 haze days (2:00 pm API 100 or above), looking back up to 60 days.
  • Older haze days are kept on purpose. After a clean spell, the factor is still ready when the haze comes back.
  • On clean days CAMS often reads high. The clean-day factor is shown for information only. School closure only happens in haze.
  • The correction runs by itself, at every forecast run.
  • Why the newest reading matters (17 Oct): CAMS said 85 for the 2:00 pm average. The real value was 150. The 7:00 am reading was already 146. Since 17 Oct the forecast starts from the newest reading.

5The range and the confidence

  • The grey band in the chart shows the typical day-to-day scatter of the factor on haze days (one standard deviation), kept between ±15% and ±40%. A single unusual day does not widen it much. On the same day the band is at least ±5.
  • About 2 days in 3 should fall inside the band. Days outside it will happen.
  • High: the same day, when the newest real reading is 9 hours old or less. Also days 1 and 2 after 7 or more haze days in both squares.
  • Medium: days 1 to 4 otherwise.
  • Low: day 5, or a day with missing CAMS data.
  • CAMS keeps today's fire level for all 5 days. It cannot know new fires, or fires put out by rain. This is why later days are less certain.

6What the CAMS model takes into account

CAMS starts a new run twice a day. Each run uses the items below.

FactorWhat CAMS usesNote for our area
Fire hotspotsSatellites measure the heat of each fire. CAMS turns the heat into an amount of smoke (GFAS fire data). Peat fires burn slowly with little heat, and cloud often hides them. So CAMS sees too little smoke. The same fire level is used for all 5 days.
Smoke heightHow high the smoke rises above each fire. Smoke high up travels far but does not reach the ground at once.
Haze seen from spaceSatellites measure how hazy the air is. Each run corrects its starting point with this. Cloud blocks this measurement too. On cloudy days the correction is weak.
WindThe ECMWF weather forecast. Wind moves the smoke from cell to cell, every hour. A south-west wind brings smoke from Sumatra. A wind from the east or north usually brings cleaner air.
RainRain washes particles out of the air. Rain forecasts for single storms are uncertain. A missed storm gives a forecast that is too high.
Mixing heightHow deep the layer of air is that mixes the smoke. A deep layer dilutes it. The layer is shallow at night and in the early morning, and deep in the afternoon.
Fixed sourcesTables of emissions from traffic, industry and power plants, as monthly averages. Daily changes, such as rush hour or a holiday, are not in the tables.
Dust and sea saltWorked out from the wind. Sea salt matters more at Kuala Selangor, near the coast.
ChemistrySome gases turn into fine particles in the air. A small part of PM2.5 in haze.

Not in the model: fires that start after the run; small local burning that satellites do not see; anything smaller than one 44 km cell, such as hills, towns or a single road.
